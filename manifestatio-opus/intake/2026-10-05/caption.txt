The most famous logo in luxury started as an anti-fake device.

In 1835 a 13-year-old left the Jura on foot and spent two years getting to Paris, where he learned to pack trunks for the rich. He noticed one flaw nobody fixed: rounded lids don't stack. In 1858 his flat-top canvas trunk fit the new age of trains and steamships perfectly.

Then came the copies, so his name went onto the canvas in 1888, and in 1896 his son Georges drew the Monogram to make fakes harder.

He didn't invent luggage. He fixed one annoying flaw right when travel changed.
