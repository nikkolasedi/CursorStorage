# Story 2 — IKEA / Gruen effect

**Generate Tue 29 Sep 2026 at 06:00 Europe/Berlin. Do not generate before then.**

## User story (keep the mechanism)

IKEA doesn't want you to find what you're looking for, at least not quickly. Ingvar Kamprad designed every store as a one-way labyrinth. You walk in for a lamp, you leave with candles, a rug, and a bookshelf you didn't know you needed. It's called the Gruen effect. Critics said the layout would frustrate customers. IKEA doesn't compete on selection. It competes on path. The store isn't a warehouse. It's a behavioral trap with Swedish meatballs.

## Sourced facts (use these; do not invent)

- One-way / fixed-path showroom is the mechanism. Ingvar Kamprad's store as a labyrinth.
- Average visit commonly cited around **2 hours** (dwell-time comparisons vs other big-box retail). Phrase: "about 2 hours".
- **~50% unplanned** — user-locked. Vox (2018) cites researchers estimating about half of purchases in general are unplanned; IKEA is the textbook Gruen case. Phrase: "roughly half". Do not invent a more precise IKEA-only percentage.
- Meatballs as dwell: Gerd Diewald, former IKEA US food head, called meatballs **"the best sofa-seller"** (Fast Company 2017 / CNBC 2019). IKEA sells **more than 1 billion** Swedish meatballs a year (CNBC 2019). Restaurant sits mid-path so shoppers stay and decide in-store.
- **FORBIDDEN:** "tripled average spending" / "tripled spend". Do not use unless a named source appears in this brief (it does not).
