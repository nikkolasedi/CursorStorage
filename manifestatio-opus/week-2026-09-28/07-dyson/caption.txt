James Dyson did not improve the vacuum bag. He killed it.

It took 5,127 prototypes, built in a backyard workshop while Deirdre's art-teacher salary kept the family going. Bags clog, airflow dies and suction fades, so he built a cyclone that needs no bag at all. Incumbents would not sell a bagless machine because bags were the real business, so he launched it himself, and he still owns the company.

The lesson: if the old product needs a refill, own the machine that doesn't.
