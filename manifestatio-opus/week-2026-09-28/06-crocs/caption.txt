The ugliest shoe on the boat became a business worth about $4.1 billion a year.

In 2002 Scott Seamans, George Boedecker and Lyndon "Duke" Hanson found a foam clog that actually gripped a wet deck. They licensed Croslite foam from a company in Quebec, built their own version and put it on a table at the Fort Lauderdale boat show, where 1,000 pairs sold out. They called it Crocs, after the crocodile, because it works on land and water. The grip, the cushioning and the easy cleaning carried it far beyond the dock.

The lesson: ugly that solves a real problem still wins.
